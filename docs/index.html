    A useful prediction starts
    with an honest evaluation.

    Can recorded browsing behaviour identify sessions that end in a purchase? We compare three standard classifiers against a majority baseline on one historical dataset.

    One row. One session. One outcome.

    Revenue = 1 means purchase; 0 means no purchase. The input values summarize a recorded session. This study supports association within this dataset, not claims about motives, conversion uplift or early-session prediction.

    THE DEPLOYED PREDICTION WORKSPACE IS A SEPARATE MODEL

    Six inputs for new sessions

    The prediction workspace accepts individual inputs and CSV files from outside this dataset. It runs a separately trained Random Forest using ProductRelated, ProductRelated_Duration, Informational, Informational_Duration, VisitorType and Weekend. Administrative and Administrative_Duration were removed from the deployed predictor at the user’s request. Their earlier eight-input results are archived for a measured comparison. No unshown features are filled with defaults. PageValues, bounce/exit rates and the technical category codes are excluded.

    This product model's test AP is 0.293, compared with the full-feature research result documented below. Its frozen validation-selected threshold is 0.12. It found 245 of 286 actual buyers, with 894 false alarms among 1139 flagged sessions. Its score is an uncalibrated model output, not a guaranteed purchase probability. The original benchmark's stronger metrics must never be attributed to this predictor.

    All three classifiers were tuned on the original training rows with the same bounded three-fold AP searches. Validation selected the model and threshold before this condition's test evaluation. The historical test split is reused from the research project, not an independent external validation. New stores, future sessions and early-session use remain unvalidated.

    Python/JavaScript parity is checked on all 3,700 validation/test rows and three constructed fresh sessions. Forest inference uses the fitted numeric/category preprocessing and float32 tree comparisons. Signed path contributions sum to score minus the mean root score; they are model attributions, not causal effects.

    Product version: predictor-b3055ee3-4cb83771. Its complete CV, selection, predictions and metrics are in results/predictor/ in the downloadable source bundle.

    02 PREPARATION

    Prepare the inputs.
    Protect the holdout.

    Every learned transformation sits inside a scikit-learn Pipeline and ColumnTransformer. Each CV fold fits its own preprocessing using only that fold’s training rows.

    Preprocessing choices & statistical rationale
    One consistent preprocessing contract
    ChoiceImplementationWhy
    Numeric missingnessTraining median, if neededRetain rows without learning from the holdout; no missing values observed here
    Categorical missingnessFixed __missing__ categoryMake missingness explicit; no missing values observed here
    Category encodingOneHotEncoder; ignore unknown categoriesCodes are labels, not distances; vocabulary is fitted on training folds
    Logistic Regression scaleStandardScaler on numeric featuresDifferent units otherwise distort regularization and optimization
    Tree numeric scaleOriginal valuesThreshold-based trees do not require standardization
    Target and row identityExplicit Revenue 0/1; source_row_id from original row orderNeither the target nor the identifier enters the predictors
    Imbalance and extremesStratification; no resampling, class weights, or outlier removalKeep the core comparison simple; inspect precision/recall and threshold choices
    !

    A useful feature is not automatically an available feature.

    UCI describes PageValues as an analytics measure associated with pages visited before transactions. That definition warrants a timing audit; it does not prove this file leaks the target. We prespecify a with/without experiment. Counts, durations, bounce and exit summaries may also develop during or after a session.

    03 THE MODELS

    Start simple.
    Earn the complexity.

    A reference and three familiar classifiers are enough to test the question. Each gets the same source rows, feature contract and evaluation protocol.

    00 / REFERENCE

    Majority baseline

    Always predicts no purchase. Establishes how far accuracy can go without detecting a single buyer.

    DummyClassifierstrategy = most_frequent
    No search

    Weakness: ignores every browsing signal.

    01 / LINEAR

    Logistic Regression

    Fits a regularized linear relationship in log odds after scaling and category encoding.

    3 candidatesC ∈ {0.1, 1, 10}
    max_iter = 2000

    Weakness: limited nonlinear interactions without engineered terms.

    02 / RULES

    Decision Tree

    Recursively splits sessions into groups using feature thresholds; gives a readable rule structure.

    6 candidatesdepth ∈ {3, 5, 8}
    min leaf ∈ {5, 20}

    Weakness: a deeper tree may overfit and vary with the sample.

    03 / BAGGING

    Random Forest

    Averages 150 randomized trees to reduce variance. A standard nonlinear comparator.

    4 candidatesdepth ∈ {8, unbounded}
    min leaf ∈ {2, 10}

    Weakness: harder to interpret as a whole than one tree or a linear model.

    No advanced boosting, class weighting, resampling, or large parameter search. random_state = 42 wherever supported. Actual parameters and fitted category vocabularies are saved in the source bundle.

    04 FAIR EVALUATION

    Three partitions.
    Three different jobs.

    We hold out 15% for test, then take 15/85 of the remaining rows for validation. Both splits are stratified with seed 42. Exact source row IDs are saved and checked for complete, disjoint coverage.

    70%TRAIN15%VALIDATE15%TEST
    Exact stratified split · random_state = 42
    PartitionSessionsPurchasesPurpose
    Train8,6301,336Fit preprocessing and tune with 3-fold CV
    Validation1,850286Select model and threshold
    Test1,850286Final frozen comparison only
    1. Tune on training only. Three-fold StratifiedKFold, shuffled, seed 42. GridSearchCV maximizes purchase average precision and refits on the full training partition. Every candidate and fold score is saved.
    2. Select on validation. Evaluate tuned models at 0.50, choose by AP with the declared simplicity tolerance, then choose the selected model’s F1 threshold on the fixed 0.05–0.95 grid.
    3. Freeze, then test. Persist the model, parameters, feature sets and threshold before test predictions. Compare all frozen variants at 0.50, plus the selected model at its chosen threshold.

    What the metrics mean

    Average precision (AP)Purchase precision summarized over recall increments across thresholds. Primary ranking metric; not trapezoidal PR-AUC.

    PrecisionOf the sessions predicted to purchase, how many actually did: TP / (TP + FP).

    RecallOf the actual purchases, how many were detected: TP / (TP + FN).

    F1Harmonic mean of precision and recall. Used for the illustrative threshold choice.

    ROC-AUCRanking discrimination between a random buyer and non-buyer across thresholds.

    AccuracyCorrect predictions / all sessions. Can reward the dominant non-purchase class.

    Precision and recall use zero_division=0. A zero displayed when no positive predictions exist is an implementation convention; precision is mathematically undefined there. Single-class ROC-AUC would be undefined and disclosed. Model scores are not claimed to be calibrated probabilities. There is no known business cost ratio.

    THE FROZEN DECISION

    Random Forest at 0.24

    Random Forest was selected with validation AP 0.725. We take the highest validation AP, then prefer the simpler model when it is within 0.005 of the highest: Logistic Regression → Decision Tree → Random Forest. This practical tolerance is not a significance test. The validation F1 grid selected 0.24; ties use the larger threshold. All six fitted variants and this decision were frozen before any test predictions. No model was refitted on validation.

    08 CONCLUSIONS & LIMITATIONS

    Signals, with boundaries.

    In this historical dataset, recorded session summaries identify purchases better than always predicting no purchase. Validation-selected Random Forest with full features achieved held-out test AP 0.726 versus baseline 0.155, and purchase F1 0.657 at the frozen 0.24 threshold. It still missed 63 of 286 test purchases. This supports session-level discrimination on this split, with clear dependence on the recorded feature set; it does not establish early-session utility or conversion uplift.

    01

    Timing is unresolved

    These are recorded session summaries. Some signals may be known only after browsing progresses or ends. Neither the full-feature nor reduced-feature experiment proves an early-session decision is possible.

    02

    One historical setting

    A random stratified split measures internal discrimination, not future-time or cross-site generalization. No external validation, conversion intervention, uplift test, or business impact study was performed.

    03

    Uncertainty remains

    Results come from one deterministic primary split and small grids. Differences are descriptive, without confidence intervals or significance tests. Duplicate and subgroup sensitivities help inspection but do not remove uncertainty.

    04

    The labels set the scope

    Revenue records purchase occurrence. The file contains no customer price/quality preferences, purchase motives or product-quality labels. Scores are uncalibrated; the F1 threshold is illustrative, without known business costs.

    Future work: collect timestamped feature snapshots, declare a real decision point and cost model, then evaluate prospectively on a later period. That would answer a different, operational question.
